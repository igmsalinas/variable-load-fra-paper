# Manuscript review

The revised manuscript presents the work as a simulation study of fault detection in a synchronous buck converter. The journal draft retains the reported aggregate results and uses healthy-validation calibration, retrospective rank selection, and the supervised comparator as distinct experimental conditions.

## Corrections made

- Derived the averaged transfer function from the small-signal output impedance and duty-cycle linearization. The model-fidelity statistic of 0.0952% applies at known simulated loads. The fault augmentation instead applies ratios at the nominal load, whose accuracy as a surrogate for faults at arbitrary loads remains unquantified.
- Removed the claim of a universal switch observability ceiling. The low-side result applies to the simulated switch configuration, and the measured high-side recall is a benchmark result. Also corrected the conversion from phase in degrees to the complex response.
- Described the 5% prevalence as assumed and distinguished prevalence-adjusted average precision from thresholded F1 at the test split's class balance. The reported SDs summarize seed variability on one split. PCA ranks chosen using labeled test scores are identified as retrospective references.
- Reframed the attention and gradient analysis as descriptive. Reduced sensitivity within the resonance band alone does not establish load invariance or the effect of a change in load.
- Removed two example traces from Figure 2(a). Their labels indicated a 47% capacitance decrease and a 215% capacitor ESR increase, outside the respective 45% and 200% maxima in Table I. The other two example traces, the healthy envelope, and the remaining panels were retained.
- Updated the literature discussion with published buck-converter work on capacitor monitoring and corrected the author and year metadata for the cited MOSFET prognostics study. The related work now describes the generic augmentation comparator as an adaptation, consistent with the broader [published CARLA method](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2024.110874). [Buck-converter monitoring study](https://doi.org/10.1109/JESTPE.2020.2964068) · [MOSFET prognostics paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110014227/downloads/20110014227.pdf)

## Items to verify before submission

1. **Freeze the experiment version.** The available implementation uses per-anchor explicit negatives, a lossless dc gain in its nominal buck model, and an additional random scaling step for positive views. The original draft described in-batch negatives, conduction-loss ratios, and Gaussian positive jitter for the variable-load experiment. The revised objective leaves the exact negative set to be confirmed. The matching experiment revision is needed to determine which objective, model, and augmentation generated the reported results. Verify the synthetic-fault severity and pairing distributions, checkpoint selection, and scoring specifications against that revision.
2. **Supply the experiment records.** The available files contain summary tables and plots. They do not establish the raw variable-load sweep identities or per-run predictions. Archive the switched model and solver and sweep settings, parameter draws, split manifest, configurations, and per-run predictions. Replace the current availability statement with a stable repository or dataset citation if the materials can be released.
3. **Verify reporting conventions from those records.** Confirm average-precision integration and ties, the threshold used by the supervised comparison, the noise sampling and recalibration protocol, and the gradient normalization and aggregation used in Figure 9. Report observed healthy-test false-positive counts at the main threshold. For any claim about improvement under load variation alone, use a matched comparison that holds the component draws and sample sizes fixed.
4. **Confirm journal metadata.** Check the spelling and diacritics of author names, the corresponding author's email, and the target journal's submission requirements. The paper is formatted using the generic IEEE journal class.
